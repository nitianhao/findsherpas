## Visual derivation

Mechanism: interpret real customer queries in the context of a retailer's catalogue, then prioritize and validate changes to search.
Scene: a product or ecommerce leader checks the agency on a laptop following an introduction; clarity in normal office light matters.
Category ruts: generic SaaS hero/card grid, and its predictable dark monospace dashboard opposite.

Grounded directions, ordered before seed assignment:
1. A retail window that reveals customer intent through changing merchandise.
2. A merchandising table built around catalogue comparison and selection.
3. A departures board routing different query classes to the right outcomes.
4. A language reference with words annotated by meaning; the query itself is the primary visual.
5. A textile sample library comparing search qualities through specific samples.
6. A rehearsal sequence where the same shopping scenario is tested and refined.
7. An exploded assembly of the search decisions, their inputs, and their dependencies.

Seed c85c00a9 assigned direction 4. The language-reference direction makes multilingual query understanding explicit without imaginary customer data. Adapt it in a royal-blue field, large humanist sans, and pale yellow meaning highlights. Query annotations must be interactive and educational. Three composition probes generated using the built-in image tool.

Catalog challengers were fused with the search use case and judged on audience identification and product clarity. Curved paper explains transformation but obscures search; cloud quarry shifts attention to an imaginary service; emission rail repeats the rejected instrument direction and hides the offer; teletext makes query interaction clear but weakens premium agency identification; multilingual silkscreen communicates languages but less of the search method; teen quiz supports interaction but misidentifies the buyer's context. None improves both axes over annotated customer language.
